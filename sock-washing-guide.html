The Sock Washing Guide

Socks are washed more often than anything else you own. A few small changes to how you do it will keep them soft, bright and elastic for years.

Before the wash

Turn every sock inside out. The inside is where skin, sweat and the smell are, so that is the side that needs the water. It also protects the outer face from pilling and keeps patterns crisp.

Pair socks loosely or put them in a mesh laundry bag. This is the only reliable cure for the missing-sock problem, and it stops fine socks stretching around the drum.

Sort by fibre and colour

  • Whites on their own, so they stay white.
  • Darks and brights together, away from whites. New dark socks shed dye for the first few washes.
  • Wool separately, on a wool or delicate cycle.
  • Muddy sports socks rinsed first, then washed with other sportswear.

Leave out the softener

Fabric softener works by coating fibres with a waxy layer. On socks, that layer blocks the fibres from absorbing and moving sweat, which is the one job a sock has. It also builds up in terry loops and makes them flat. If socks feel stiff, use less detergent and add an extra rinse.

Drying

High heat is what turns a snug cuff into a slack one. The elastane in socks is damaged above about sixty degrees, and tumble dryers pass that easily. Hang socks by the toe, not the cuff, so the weight of the water does not stretch the elastic. Lay wool socks flat. If you must use a dryer, choose the lowest setting and take them out while slightly damp.

Fixing common problems

Grey whites

Soak for an hour in warm water with an oxygen-based whitener, then wash as normal. Avoid chlorine bleach, which yellows elastane.

Lingering smell

Smell that survives a wash is bacteria living in detergent residue. Wash again with half the usual detergent and a cup of white vinegar in the softener drawer, then dry in fresh air.

Pilling

Small bobbles form where fibres rub. Washing inside out prevents most of it. A fabric shaver removes what is there.

Shrunken wool

Wool that has felted in a hot wash cannot be fully restored. Soaking in cool water with a little hair conditioner and stretching gently while damp will recover some size.

How often

After every wear for cotton and synthetic socks. Merino resists odour and can be aired overnight and worn a second day if it was not a strenuous one. Owning enough pairs to rotate means each pair is washed less often and lasts longer.

Key points

  • Turn socks inside out before washing.
  • Thirty degrees suits nearly every sock.
  • Never use fabric softener on sports or wool socks.
  • Air dry; heat destroys elastic.
